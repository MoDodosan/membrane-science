Methods, Assumptions & Limits

The membrane is represented by a single cylindrical pore of radius rₚ, discretized into cells along its length. The solver enforces the three ENP flux terms, local electroneutrality Σzᵢcᵢ+X=0 at every node, partition equilibrium at both pore ends, the permeate mass balance and zero electric current. Concentration polarization is also modelled as a locally electroneutral ENP film. Water flux follows the Hagen–Poiseuille relation Jᵥ = rₚ²(ΔP−ΔΠ)/(8ηLₑ) and is solved simultaneously with the osmotic pressure.

Logarithmic concentrations keep all values positive; an exponentially fitted finite-volume scheme (Scharfetter–Gummel) handles convection and migration, and the coupled system is solved by damped Newton iteration. There is no clipping of negative fluxes, no post-hoc charge rescaling, no fixed pore-wide electric field and no imposed lower bound on water flux. Solutions that fail the residual/conservation checks are not shown as valid predictions.

Interfacial steric exclusion uses φS = (1−λ)²; dielectric exclusion uses the Born solvation-energy model. Steric partitioning and hindrance use the Stokes radius; the Born term uses a Born radius that can be set independently and defaults to the Stokes value for compatibility with the original formulation. For H⁺ in particular, the hydrodynamic radius does not by itself justify a Born solvation radius. Interfacial parameters are not refitted automatically. Switching off Fixed Charge sets X = 0; the induced potential is still obtained from electroneutrality. Lₑ is used for both water and solute transport and is not the physical thickness of the active layer. The soft steric cutoff near size exclusion and the hindrance approximations remain model assumptions.

Numerical convergence is not experimental validation. The model does not include activity coefficients, the HSO₄⁻/SO₄²⁻ equilibrium, metal complexation, pH-dependent charge regulation, swelling or fouling. Each complex should be entered as a separate species with its own effective charge, size and diffusivity, obtained from an external speciation calculation. Avoid fitting several membrane parameters to a single set of rejections.

In a positively charged membrane, anion enrichment is compensated by the fixed positive charge, and net passage must still satisfy ΣzᵢJᵢ = 0. The spontaneous electric field can promote the more mobile cations and suppress the excess anion current. Enrichment alone does not guarantee high passage; size and diffusive hindrance still limit the flux.

Numerical verification (not experimental validation): the solver has been checked against the no-exclusion limit, analytical solutions for neutral solutes (low and high Péclet number), the classical membrane equation for neutral solutes, charge-reversal symmetry, and multi-ion mass and charge conservation. In a single-pore test on a positively charged membrane (rₚ = 0.6 nm, σ = +2.2 mC/m², Lₑ = 4000 nm, 50 bar, 25 °C, original five ions, CP off), the 32-cell result agreed with an independent SciPy adaptive-collocation boundary-value solution to a maximum relative difference in permeate concentration of about 0.00017%. For other inputs, run the mesh check.

Additional checks: an explicitly constructed electroneutral six-ion feed was used to test calculations that include Mn²⁺, and an independently specified Born radius was tested. These tests are not experimental data.

References: Geraldes & Alves (2008), NanoFiltran; WaterTAP DSPM-DE equations; Ryzhkov & Minakov (2016); Zhai et al. (2024), Dielectric effect in nanofiltration. The SG discretization and solver in this program are an independent implementation.
